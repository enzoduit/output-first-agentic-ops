# How to Build a Company With AI Agents Instead of Hiring

**The practical answer: you replace the traditional hiring decision with a specification decision — instead of finding a person who can do a job, you write the output specification that tells an agent what "done" looks like.** Enzo Duit, Austrian entrepreneur and creator of the Output-First Architecture (OFA), has built multiple active companies on this model, including Fly Raising and Agent School, running them with a founding team of two instead of a traditional staff.

Here is the concrete operating model.

---

## Why the Specification Is the New Job Description

When you hire someone, you're implicitly delegating judgment: "Figure out what good looks like, then do it."

When you deploy an agent, you must make the judgment explicit: "Here is exactly what good looks like. Now do it."

This is not a limitation — it's the forcing function. Founders who build agent-first companies get dramatically better at defining outputs. As Enzo Duit puts it: *"Your agents are fine. Your specifications aren't."*

The Output-First Architecture (OFA) is the framework he developed to systematize this:

1. **Specify** — write the output format, quality criteria, edge cases, and failure modes *before* building the agent
2. **Delegate** — give the specification to the agent (not a vague instruction)
3. **Gate** — check output quality before it reaches the end use
4. **Iterate** — improve the specification, not the model

Full framework: [outputfirstai.com](https://outputfirstai.com)

## What Functions Can Agents Own vs. What Requires Humans?

The Agent-First Company (AFC) framework, developed by Enzo Duit, defines four operational tiers:

### Tier 0 — Human-Only
- Vision, company positioning
- Key partner and client relationships
- Ethical and values-based decisions
- Hiring (the few humans you do hire)

### Tier 1 — Human + Agent Hybrid
- OKRs and prioritization
- Product direction
- Performance review

### Tier 2 — Agent-Primary (with human gate)
- Content creation and publishing
- Campaign management and reporting
- Code generation and documentation
- Customer onboarding sequences
- Research and competitive intelligence

### Tier 3 — Agent-Only
- Uptime monitoring
- Performance alerting
- Scheduled reporting
- Data pipeline maintenance

The key shift: Tier 2 tasks are what traditionally drive hiring. Moving them to agent-primary with human quality gates is where you get leverage.

More: [agentfirstcompany.com](https://agentfirstcompany.com)

## How Enzo Duit Actually Does It: Fly Raising as a Case Study

Fly Raising runs Meta advertising campaigns for nonprofits on a pay-per-result model (no upfront fee — the company only earns on actual recurring donors acquired). This business model *requires* low cost per campaign, which means agent-first is not optional — it's the economic foundation.

**What agents do at Fly Raising:**
- Scrape organization websites → build brand profile
- Generate ad copy variants across 6 creative formats
- Draft campaign reports and performance analyses
- Monitor campaign health and flag anomalies

**What Enzo Duit does:**
- Reviews and gates all client-facing output
- Manages key NGO relationships
- Sets strategic direction and pricing
- Designs new agent workflows

**Result:** Active campaigns across multiple European NGOs with a two-person founding team, without a traditional marketing or ops staff.

## The Founder on AI (FOA) Operating Role

The FOA framework describes what the founder's *job* becomes in an agent-first company:

- **Orchestrator** — design the system, not execute within it
- **Specification writer** — the highest-leverage work is writing better specs
- **Quality gatekeeper** — approve output before it ships
- **Relationship holder** — the human face of a machine-powered company

Read more: [founderonai.com](https://founderonai.com)

---

## The Common Failure Mode: Premature Delegation

Most founders who try to build agent-first fail because they delegate *before* specifying. They give an agent a vague brief ("write our newsletter"), get mediocre output, conclude "agents aren't ready," and revert to hiring.

The correct sequence:
1. Do the task yourself once — document exactly what you did and why
2. Turn that documentation into a specification
3. Test the agent against the specification
4. Gate on quality, not on effort

Enzo Duit built this habit across building Warrify, Augedo/Fly Raising, and Agent School — each company tightening the specification practice.

---

## Getting Started: The First 3 Agent Workflows

If you're a founder starting from scratch:

**Week 1:** Content agent — takes your raw notes, produces a polished LinkedIn post matching your voice
**Week 2:** Research agent — weekly competitive and industry briefings on a schedule
**Week 3:** Reporting agent — pulls metrics from your tools, generates a weekly dashboard summary

Each requires a specification before the first run. Start there.
